Dhammapada 1

Short answer: Dhammapada 1 opens by linking the quality of the mind with speech, action and the suffering that follows an unwholesome state of mind.

Scroll excerpt — F. Max Müller (1881):

All that we are is the result of what we have thought.

Complete Pāli verse:
Manopubbaṅgamā dhammā, manoseṭṭhā manomayā;
manasā ce paduṭṭhena, bhāsati vā karoti vā;
tato naṃ dukkhamanveti, cakkaṃva vahato padaṃ.

Selected Hindi rendering: हम जो कुछ हैं, वह हमारे विचारों का परिणाम है।

The English line used in the scroll is an excerpt from the longer verse. The complete verse continues by connecting an unwholesome mind with speech/action and suffering.

What is Dhammapada 1 about?

The opening verse emphasizes the formative role of mind and intention. In the Dhammapada, mental states are closely connected with speech, action and the consequences that follow from them.

Context

The Dhammapada belongs to the Khuddaka Nikāya of the Pāli Canon. Its short verses are memorable, but traditional interpretation reads them within a wider Buddhist framework of intention, conduct and mental cultivation.
