Dhammapada 5

Short answer: Dhammapada 5 says cycles of hatred are not ended by more hatred; the alternative is non-hatred, a principle the verse calls an ancient teaching.

Scroll excerpt — F. Max Müller (1881):

Hatred does not cease by hatred at any time: hatred ceases by love.

Complete Pāli verse:
Na hi verena verāni sammant’ idha kudācana;
averena ca sammanti, esa dhammo sanantano.

Selected Hindi rendering: घृणा से घृणा कभी समाप्त नहीं होती; प्रेम से ही समाप्त होती है।

The short English feed text omits the closing phrase that identifies this as an ancient principle.

What does Dhammapada 5 mean?

The verse teaches that hostility is not ended by answering hostility with more hostility. The Pāli emphasizes avera, non-hatred or freedom from enmity, as the alternative that interrupts the cycle.

What does “non-hatred” mean here?

The Pāli term avera is more precise than simply “love.” It points to the absence of hostility or enmity. That nuance is one reason translations of Dhammapada 5 can look different while expressing the same underlying contrast.

Context

This is one of the Dhammapada’s best-known teachings on anger and conflict. It is useful as a short reflection, but its fuller meaning sits within Buddhist teachings on intention, conduct and liberation from harmful mental states.

Source and edition

The English corpus uses F. Max Müller’s 1881 public-domain translation. Verify the edition at Project Gutenberg.
