Bhagavad Gita 2:47

Short answer: Bhagavad Gita 2:47 teaches disciplined action without attachment to the result, while also warning against using detachment as an excuse for inaction.

Scroll excerpt — Besant-era public-domain English:

Thy business is with the action only, never with its fruits.

Complete Sanskrit verse:
कर्मण्येवाधिकारस्ते मा फलेषु कदाचन ।
मा कर्मफलहेतुर्भूर्मा ते सङ्गोऽस्त्वकर्मणि ॥ २-४७ ॥

IAST transliteration:
karmaṇy evādhikāras te mā phaleṣu kadācana |
mā karma-phala-hetur bhūr mā te saṅgo ’stv akarmaṇi ||

Selected Hindi rendering: तुम्हारा अधिकार केवल कर्म करने में है, उसके फलों में कभी नहीं।

The short English line used in the scroll is an excerpt, not the complete verse. The second half continues the teaching by rejecting fruit as the motive and attachment to inaction.

What does Bhagavad Gita 2:47 mean?

In this passage Krishna directs Arjuna toward disciplined action without making attachment to the result the basis of action. The verse is often summarized as focusing on the work rather than the reward, but the surrounding dialogue is broader: it concerns duty, judgment, self-mastery and the relationship between action and attachment.

Why this verse is often misunderstood

The verse is sometimes reduced to “results do not matter.” That is too simple. The teaching is about acting without possessive attachment to outcomes, not about ignoring consequences or refusing responsibility.

Context

Bhagavad Gita 2:47 appears during Krishna’s response to Arjuna’s crisis about whether and how to act. It is not a teaching that outcomes never matter; it addresses how a person relates inwardly to action and its fruits.
