What is the Dhammapada?

The Dhammapada is a well-known collection of Buddhist verses preserved in the Pāli Canon. Its concise teachings address the mind, intention, ethical conduct, anger, discipline, heedfulness and liberation.

What does “Dhammapada” mean?

The title is commonly rendered along the lines of “Path of Dhamma” or “Verses of Dhamma.” Exact English wording varies because the Pāli word pada can carry several related senses, including a word, verse, step or path.

Where is the Dhammapada in the Pāli Canon?

The Dhammapada belongs to the Khuddaka Nikāya, a collection within the Sutta Piṭaka of the Theravāda Pāli Canon. Its verse format has made many passages especially memorable and widely quoted.

Pāli and translation

Pāli preserves the canonical language of the text. English translation makes the teachings accessible to a wider audience, while reading the Pāli can reveal terms and nuances that do not map neatly into a single English phrase.
