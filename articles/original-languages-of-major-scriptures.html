Original Languages of Major Scriptures: What Readers Should Know

A practical guide to Arabic, Sanskrit, Pāli, Hebrew, Gurmukhi and Classical Chinese in major religious texts, and why original-language access changes how translation should be understood.

Published Nov 2, 2026Source-labeled guide

Why original language matters

Translation makes scripture accessible, but it always involves choices. Words carry grammar, sound, cultural associations and multiple possible meanings that may not map neatly into English.

Qur’an: Arabic

The Qur’an is read in Arabic, and Muslims distinguish the Arabic revelation from translations of its meaning. English translations such as Pickthall can be useful, but they are still translations.

Bhagavad Gita: Sanskrit

The Bhagavad Gita is a Sanskrit text. Terms such as dharma, yoga, karma and ātman carry philosophical meanings that often require more than a one-word English equivalent.

Dhammapada: Pāli

The Dhammapada is preserved in Pāli within the Theravāda canon. Translation choices can shape how readers understand terms related to mind, suffering, conduct and liberation.

Tanakh: Biblical Hebrew

The Tanakh is primarily associated with Hebrew, with some Aramaic in the broader Hebrew Bible. Hebrew word roots, poetry and parallelism can produce nuances that differ across English translations.

Guru Granth Sahib: multiple languages, Gurmukhi script

The Guru Granth Sahib is written in Gurmukhi script and includes compositions drawing on several linguistic traditions. Script and language are therefore related but not identical concepts.

Tao Te Ching: Classical Chinese

The Tao Te Ching is written in Classical Chinese, whose compressed style can support multiple interpretations. That is one reason English translations can differ sharply while still engaging the same source text.

How Scroll My Faith uses language switching

The reader keeps the citation visible while allowing language changes where the corpus supports them. The goal is not to imply that a translated line is interchangeable with the original, but to make the relationship more visible.
