Bhagavad Gita 2:47 and the question of action

Thy business is with the action only, never with its fruits.

The Bhagavad Gita begins with a crisis of action. Arjuna is not simply asking what he wants to do; he is struggling with what he ought to do. Verse 2:47 is often read as a teaching on disciplined action without attachment to the outcome.

The point is not passivity or indifference to consequences. The surrounding dialogue places the verse inside a larger discussion of duty, judgment, self-mastery and the relationship between action and attachment.

Purpose is not just personal preference

Modern discussions of purpose often focus on finding a satisfying career or personal calling. The Gita’s treatment is broader and more demanding. It asks how action, duty, self-mastery and spiritual understanding fit together.

Reading the surrounding dialogue helps prevent the verse from being reduced to a productivity slogan.
