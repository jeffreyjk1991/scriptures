What is the Tanakh?

Tanakh is a common Jewish name for the Hebrew Bible. It refers to the collection of Jewish scriptures organized into three major divisions: Torah, Nevi’im and Ketuvim.

What does “Tanakh” mean?

Tanakh is formed from the Hebrew initials of its three divisions: Torah (Teaching), Nevi’im (Prophets) and Ketuvim (Writings). Vowels are added when the acronym is pronounced as a word. This is why spellings such as “Tanakh” and “Tanach” can both appear in English.

Is the Tanakh the Hebrew Bible?

“Tanakh” and “Hebrew Bible” generally refer to the same core collection of Jewish scriptures, although terminology, book order and interpretive context differ from how Christian traditions organize and describe the Old Testament.
